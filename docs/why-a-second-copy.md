# Why you need a second copy — the receipts

Loss-aversion is the honest reason people back up. These are **real, documented cases**, used on the
marketing site (kept short there; the detail lives here). **Framing rule — never attack Apple.** The
point is not "Apple is bad"; it's that *depending on a single account for your only copy is a structural
risk*, true of any provider. iCloud is a brilliant **sync** service — and sync is not a backup you own.

## 1. Wiped in minutes — the only copies, gone
August 2012: Wired journalist **Mat Honan** was hacked. Attackers social-engineered **Amazon and Apple
support** to reach his iCloud account, then used Find My to **remotely wipe his MacBook, iPhone, and
iPad**. It permanently erased roughly **a year and a half of photos of his young daughter** plus photos
of deceased relatives — the only copies he had. The failure wasn't malice; it was a *support-process
exploit* plus the fact that his devices + iCloud were a single, remotely-wipeable point of failure.
- Wired — "How Apple and Amazon Security Flaws Led to My Epic Hacking": https://www.wired.com/2012/08/apple-amazon-mat-honan-hacking/
- Also covered by CNN and ProPublica.
- **What micloud sells:** a second copy that lives somewhere a remote wipe can't reach.

## 2. Locked out, with little recourse
Apple — like any account provider — can **lock or disable an Apple Account** when its systems flag
activity as fraud or a security risk. It's a safeguard that occasionally catches innocent people.
Documented cases describe users losing access to **terabytes of iCloud photos, messages, and thousands
of dollars of purchases** after something as small as a flagged gift card, then getting vague
explanations and a "create a new Apple ID" suggestion that itself risks their standing — a catch-22,
because recovery portals need an active login.
- Apple Support — "If your Apple Account is locked, not active, or disabled": https://support.apple.com/en-us/102640
- **What micloud sells:** if your only copy lives *inside* an account, you're one false-positive from losing it.

## 3. Sync is not a backup
iCloud mirrors your *current* state in real time. That's the feature — and the risk:
- **Delete propagates.** Remove a photo/file on one device and it's gone from all of them. iCloud keeps
  deleted items ~30 days, then they're permanently gone.
- **Ransomware syncs up.** If malware encrypts your local files, the corrupted versions sync to iCloud
  within minutes and overwrite the good copies.
- The common real version: people free phone space by deleting photos assuming "iCloud has them," and
  find after 30 days they're unrecoverable.
- Source: MakeUseOf — "I learned the hard way that cloud sync isn't a backup":
  https://www.makeuseof.com/i-learned-the-hard-way-that-cloud-sync-isnt-a-backup/
- The **3-2-1 rule**: 3 copies, 2 media, 1 off-site & independent of your live sync.
- **What micloud sells:** the independent, off-sync copy the 3-2-1 rule asks for.
